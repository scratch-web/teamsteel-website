1Name the place and value of a digit
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place Value

Worked example

4.286 = 4 + 0.2 + 0.08 + 0.006

Place value chart for 4.286
TensOnesTenthsHundredthsThousandths
04286
1011/101/1001/1000
  1. Find the decimal point. The ones place is immediately to its left.
  2. To the right, name the places in order: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.
  3. In 4.286, the 2 is in the tenths place and has a value of 0.2 (2/10). The 8 has a value of 0.08 (8/100). The 6 has a value of 0.006 (6/1000).
  4. Check that each digit matches its column in the place value chart below.

Tip: The place is the name of the column; the value is how much the digit represents. There is no “oneths” place.

Try it

In 7.305, name the place and value of the 5.

Check your answer

Thousandths; 0.005 = 5/1000.

2Understand the ten-times pattern
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place Value

Worked example

0.7 = 10 × 0.07; 0.07 = 10 × 0.007

  1. Compare the same digit in neighboring places: 7 tenths, 7 hundredths, and 7 thousandths.
  2. One tenth contains 10 hundredths. So 7 tenths is ten times as much as 7 hundredths.
  3. One hundredth contains 10 thousandths. So 7 hundredths is ten times as much as 7 thousandths.
  4. For the same digit, each place to the right represents one tenth of its value in the place immediately to the left.

Tip: Compare the values of the digits, not just the digits. The 7 stays the same, but its value changes with its place.

Try it

How many times as much is the 4 in 0.4 as the 4 in 0.04?

Check your answer

Ten times as much: 0.4 = 10 × 0.04.

3Read and write decimal forms
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place Value

Worked example

3.042 = 3 + 4/100 + 2/1000

  1. Read the whole-number part: three.
  2. Use “and” for the decimal point when reading the number in word form.
  3. The last digit is in the thousandths place. The decimal part is forty-two thousandths.
  4. Word form: three and forty-two thousandths. Expanded form: 3 + 4/100 + 2/1000, or 3 + 0.04 + 0.002.
  5. Keep the zero in the tenths place when writing 3.042; it holds a place with no tenths.

Tip: 3.042 and 3.42 are different amounts. A zero between the decimal point and a nonzero digit matters.

Try it

Write 6.208 in word form and expanded form.

Check your answer

Six and two hundred eight thousandths; 6 + 2/10 + 8/1000 = 6 + 0.2 + 0.008.

4Rename equivalent decimals
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place Value

Worked example

0.6 = 0.60 = 0.600

  1. Think of 0.6 as 6 tenths of the same whole.
  2. Split each tenth into 10 hundredths: 6 tenths equals 60 hundredths.
  3. Split each hundredth into 10 thousandths: 60 hundredths equals 600 thousandths.
  4. Adding zeros at the end of the decimal part does not change the amount: 0.6 = 0.60 = 0.600.

Tip: Zeros at the end of a decimal can keep the value the same. Inserting a zero before a nonzero decimal digit can change the value: 0.6 ≠ 0.06.

Try it

Fill in: 0.32 = ___ thousandths. Are 2.5 and 2.500 equal?

Check your answer

320 thousandths; yes, 2.5 = 2.500.

5Compare and order decimals
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place ValueMath Antics: Comparing Decimal Numbers

Worked example

0.47 > 0.407 because 0.470 > 0.407

  1. Line up the decimal points so the same places line up.
  2. Rename 0.47 as 0.470. Both numbers are now easy to read in thousandths.
  3. Compare from left to right: the ones are equal and the tenths are equal.
  4. The hundredths differ: 7 hundredths is greater than 0 hundredths. Stop at the first different place.
  5. Write 0.47 > 0.407. To order several decimals, use the same process from least to greatest.

Tip: More decimal digits does not mean a greater number. Compare place by place. The open side of > or < faces the greater value.

Try it

Order from least to greatest: 0.8, 0.078, 0.807.

Check your answer

0.078 < 0.8 < 0.807. Rename 0.8 as 0.800 to compare.

6Use a number line to check
Watch Khan AcademyWatch Math Antics: Decimal Place Value

Worked example

0.243 is between 0.24 and 0.25

  1. Find the two neighboring hundredths: 0.24 and 0.25.
  2. Divide that interval into 10 equal steps. Each step is 0.001, one thousandth.
  3. Count three steps after 0.240: 0.241, 0.242, 0.243.
  4. Check: 0.243 is closer to 0.24 than to 0.25. Numbers farther right are greater.

Tip: Name the size of each step before counting. Ten equal steps from 0.24 to 0.25 are thousandths, not tenths.

Try it

What number is six thousandth-size steps after 0.510?

Check your answer

0.516. It is between 0.51 and 0.52.

Helping at home

Ask: “What is this digit worth?” “How do you know those are equal?” and “Which place helped you compare?” Let your child explain. Pause the video and try an example together.
